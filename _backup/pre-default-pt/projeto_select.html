The Challenge

Select — From print magazine to digital experience


From paper to screen: turning an editorial publication into a digital experience. For two years, I worked on the digital version of Select magazine, taking part in the process of transforming a printed publication into a reading experience adapted to the digital environment. The challenge went beyond reproducing pages: it meant rethinking layouts, adapting content, turning graphic elements into interactive experiences and preserving the magazine's editorial identity across different devices.


Select had an editorial identity built around the printed experience. Its pages combined text, photographs, illustrations, charts and visual compositions that were part of the publication's personality. With the move to the digital environment, there was an opportunity to bring this content into new formats and explore possibilities that paper did not allow. The work consisted of preserving the magazine's visual and editorial essence, while understanding how each piece of content could work better on a screen. This meant rethinking how information was presented, how readers moved through articles and how digital resources could enrich the reading experience.


The main question was understanding that the digital experience has its own characteristics and should not be treated as a simple reproduction of the printed version. Unlike paper, screens come in varied sizes, have viewing limitations and offer different forms of interaction. A composition that works perfectly on a printed page can become hard to read or difficult to navigate on a mobile device. From this, the project presented some central challenges: Layout adaptation: reorganizing content for different screen sizes without losing the editorial identity. Legibility: ensuring reading comfort in digital environments. Interactivity: turning static elements into explorable experiences. Navigation: creating an intuitive and consistent experience. Editorial experience: preserving the publication's personality while taking advantage of digital possibilities. The goal was to find the balance between editorial fidelity, usability and innovation.



The adaptation process meant analyzing the content of each issue and identifying how it could be better presented in the digital environment. Rather than simply shrinking the printed pages, it was necessary to evaluate the structure of each composition, the importance of the information and the possibilities for interaction. This work involved three main fronts:

  1. 01

    Re-layout and content adaptation

    Reorganizing editorial elements to improve reading and viewing on smaller screens.

  2. 02

    Interactivity and data visualization

    Turning charts and other visual elements into interactive features, taking advantage of the digital medium.

  3. 03

    Navigation and reading experience

    Attention to how content is organized, to visual consistency and to how the reader interacted with the publication.

Each decision had to respect both editorial needs and the particularities of the digital experience. Main design considerations:

  • Reorganizing text, images and graphic elements.
  • Typography adjustments to improve legibility.
  • Review of margins, spacing and proportions.
  • Preserving the publication's visual identity.
  • Adapting compositions to the different screen formats.

The goal was an experience that kept the magazine's editorial character but was naturally suited to digital reading.

Learnings

The digital environment brought the possibility of turning traditional graphic elements into more dynamic experiences. Charts and infographics that once presented all the information statically began to offer interaction, allowing the reader to explore certain data more actively. This transformation required thinking not only about the appearance of the elements, but also about how people would interact with them. This project broadened my view of the role of UX Design in products whose content is the main protagonist. I learned that a good digital adaptation requires understanding not only how an interface works, but also the nature of the information being presented. I understood that preserving a visual identity does not mean keeping every original decision. Often, it is necessary to deconstruct a composition in order to rebuild it in a way better suited to the context of use. I also realized that interactivity only adds value when there is a clear purpose behind it. Not every piece of content needs animations or clickable elements; what matters most is understanding how technology can make comprehension easier and enrich the experience. This work strengthened my ability to connect design, content and technology, developing solutions that respect the essence of a publication while exploring the possibilities of a new medium.
